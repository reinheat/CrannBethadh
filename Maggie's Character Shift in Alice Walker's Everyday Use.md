# Maggie's Character Shift in Alice Walker's Everyday Use

In [[Alice Walker]]’s, “[[Everyday Use]],” details of action, appearance, and speech reveal [[Maggie]]’s character shift from a hurt and docile girl in the beginning to a resolute woman willing to stand for what she believes is right in the end. In the beginning, Maggie is characterized as someone stuck in her past, unable to escape her past [[trauma]]. Mama describes Maggie as “chin on chest, eyes on ground, feet in shuffle, ever since the fire that burned the other house to the ground” (Walker 316). This description of her movements tell us that she is scarred by the past she’s lived through, she moves like a “lame animal[...] a dog run over” (316), we can infer from what’s said that she was scarred by the flames, both physically and mentally. As the story progresses, we get hints that Maggie is more resolute than she seems. When [[Dee]] asks for the quilts Mama promised to give to Maggie we hear “something fall in the kitchen and [...] the kitchen door slammed” (320). This sudden burst of action reveals a hidden aspect of Maggie’s character, despite the fact that she’s characterized as being envious of her sister she is willing to stand for herself. By the end, we see this change in her personality, we see her more resolute. After a long argument between Mama and Dee, Maggie says “She can have them, Mama, [...] I can ‘member Grandma Dee without the quilts” (321). This moment of resolute defiance characterizes Maggie as someone who’s willing to stand up for her [[heritage]] and what she believes is right. Maggie’s shift in characters suggests that even when times are hard and you’re trapped in the past, you can always stand up for what you believe is right.

Work Cited
Walker, Alice. “Everyday Use.” In Love & Trouble: Stories of Black Women. New York: 
Harcourt, 1973.

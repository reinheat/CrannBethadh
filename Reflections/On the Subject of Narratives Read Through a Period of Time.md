I'm placed in a position where I have the good fortune of having a piece of media that I read and consumed as I grew as a person. That media is a mixture of a couple, as I finished and started new things, but most notably it is [[Lord of the Mysteries]], [[Mother of Learning]], and a little bit of [[Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint]]. Each of these stories is quite long and are on their own very good works of fiction — no one can doubt or complain about that. But I feel this kind of nostalgia towards each of these series — that I can return to them in a sense of longing, that as I grew, so too did my understanding of the stories, that throughout my high school life and throughout my burgeoning adolescence, I was able to basically grow up and have a story long enough that it would grow with me.

The reason I am thinking about this is a short video called [[Dowsing|dowsing]], which is a very beautiful kind of music-box representation of the Lord of the Mysteries series, and every time I watch it, I can't help but cry, because it tells the story of [[Klein Moretti|Klein]]'s life, and through it I am reminded of my life that I lived while reading Klein's life. I am reminded that throughout my life it was a constant I could return to. In Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint, [[Kim Dokja]], the main character of that series, experiences something akin to this: he has this serialized web novel that he reads called [[Three Ways to Survive in a Ruined World|Three Ways to Survive the Apocalypse]], and he suffers a lot through his life, but this series is always there for him. And I feel that is what Lord of the Mysteries, Mother of Learning, and for a little bit Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint are for me. They are these kinds of touchstones that I can look back on and feel nostalgia towards. I can say that this is a new feeling — that I want to return to a book, that I want to return to a world in which I know the story. I've returned to Lord of the Mysteries Volume 2 not because I want to be wowed by a story I do not know — of course not. I know what's going to happen. I know that Klein as [[Sherlock Moriarty]] will eventually suffer in the Great Smog of [[Backlund]] and become a pirate and go on these grand adventures. But it is this — what I thought was mundane in the moment. I can tell you I did not enjoy the mundane aspect of solving mysteries. But I can tell you now that I feel lonely without having that series there. I know that one day it will be animated and adapted into a new medium, but it was special in the fact that it was mine first. And not animated. And there's an aspect of me that wants to share this with people. It's not for a lack of trying. I have tried. But it's a unique longing — a unique, almost selfish one — that makes me want to keep it for myself, I suppose.
